# Pause Before You Pay, Click or Share: A Cybercrime Reporting Guide for India

The internet helps us learn, communicate, shop, make payments and stay connected. But the same digital spaces can also be misused. A person may receive a fake payment request, lose access to an account, find a duplicate social-media profile, face online threats or discover that private content has been shared without consent. In a stressful moment, it can be difficult to know what to do first. A few calm and practical steps can help protect accounts, preserve useful evidence and make an official report.

This guide is an educational awareness resource for students and the public in India. It does not replace police assistance, professional legal advice or the current instructions on government websites.

## Recognise common cybercrime situations

**Online financial fraud** can involve unauthorised UPI transfers, fake payment requests, deceptive links, fraudulent customer-care numbers or messages that pressure a person to act quickly. A caller may pretend to be from a bank, delivery service or government office. A genuine bank or government employee should not need a person's OTP, UPI PIN or password.

**Account hacking** occurs when someone gains unauthorised access to an email, social-media, messaging or other online account. Warning signs may include unexpected login alerts, changed recovery details, messages that the account owner did not send or contacts receiving unusual requests for money.

**Identity theft and impersonation** involve misuse of another person's identifying information or pretending to be that person online. A fake profile may use a person's photograph or name to contact friends, request money or damage their reputation. Preserve the profile address and report it to the platform.

**Online harassment and threats** may include repeated unwanted messages, stalking, blackmail, abusive posts or threats. The affected person should not be blamed. If there is an immediate threat of physical harm, contact local police or emergency services rather than relying only on an online form.

**Non-consensual sharing of private content** is a serious privacy and safety issue. Do not download, forward or repost intimate material. If a child may be depicted, do not save, copy, screenshot or share the material. Report it through official channels and seek appropriate support.

## What to do immediately

First, stop the incident from causing further harm. If money has been transferred or debited through a cyber financial fraud, call the national helpline **1930 immediately**. Also contact the bank, wallet or payment provider through a trusted official channel, such as its official app or website. Do not use a phone number supplied by the suspicious caller or message.

Second, secure affected accounts. Use a trusted device and the service's official recovery process. Change compromised passwords, sign out unknown sessions, remove unfamiliar recovery methods and enable multi-factor authentication. If the same password was reused on other services, change it there too. Never share an OTP, PIN, CVV, password or remote-access code with someone who contacts you unexpectedly.

Third, preserve evidence. Useful information may include transaction references, dates and times, screenshots, message records, email details, profile names, URLs, phone numbers and complaint acknowledgement numbers. Keep original messages and files where safe. Do not edit or delete the original evidence. In cases involving intimate content, preserve safe details such as the URL, account handle and message text; do not download or forward the content itself.

## How to report in India

The Government of India's **National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal** is available at [https://cybercrime.gov.in/](https://cybercrime.gov.in/). The portal provides routes for different types of cybercrime, including online financial fraud, hacking, mobile and social-media crimes, and crimes involving women and children. Follow the current instructions on the portal, select the category that best matches the incident and provide accurate information. Keep the acknowledgement or reference details for follow-up.

For financial cyber fraud, the 1930 helpline is an urgent reporting channel. Calling the helpline does not remove the need to follow the current portal instructions. Contact your bank or payment provider as well. Do not assume that a complaint automatically reverses a transaction or guarantees recovery of money.

If the incident includes immediate physical danger, threats or ongoing harassment, contact local police or emergency services as appropriate. An online portal is not a substitute for urgent in-person assistance. Report suspicious phone numbers, URLs or identifiers only through the official facilities provided by the portal.

## Why evidence matters

A clear timeline can help explain what happened. Write down when the first suspicious contact occurred, what happened next, when money was transferred (if relevant), which accounts or services were affected and when you contacted the bank or platform. Keep the transaction reference and official complaint acknowledgement. Share only information requested through legitimate reporting channels. Never publish sensitive identity or banking details on social media.

## Basic cyber-law awareness

India's Information Technology Act, 2000 includes provisions that may be relevant to cyber incidents. Section 66 concerns specified computer-related offences. Section 66C addresses identity theft involving another person's electronic signature, password or unique identification feature. Section 66D concerns cheating by personation using a computer resource. Section 66E addresses specified violations of privacy. Sections 67 and 67A concern obscene and sexually explicit material in electronic form, while Section 67B addresses specified material depicting children in sexually explicit acts.

These are simplified descriptions, not a legal opinion. The exact law that applies depends on the facts, current legislation and legal assessment. Do not rely on a chatbot or social-media post for a section number, punishment or legal conclusion. Check the latest official text on [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/) and consult a qualified legal professional when needed.

## Build safer digital habits

Pause before clicking a link or approving a payment. Verify requests through a separate, trusted channel. Use unique passwords and multi-factor authentication. Keep devices and applications updated. Review account recovery options and app permissions. Be cautious of urgency, secrecy, threats and offers that sound too good to be true. Do not install remote-access apps at the request of an unknown caller.

AI tools can help students organise research, draft awareness content and generate checklists. However, AI can also produce outdated helpline numbers, invented legal sections, incorrect penalties or unsafe advice. Compare answers from multiple tools, verify each important claim against official sources and document errors. Do not upload real victim records, private chats, passwords, company data or sensitive personal information to an AI service.

Cyber safety is a shared responsibility. Respond quickly, preserve evidence carefully, use official channels and ask for help when needed. The most important rule is simple: **pause, verify and report**.
