# AI Prompt Library

Use these prompts with fictional or public, non-sensitive information only. Do not upload real victim records, private chats, account details, passwords, OTPs or confidential data.

## Prompt 1 — Research cybercrime types
"You are a cybersecurity awareness research assistant. Explain online financial fraud, account hacking, identity theft, online harassment and non-consensual sharing of private content for an Indian student audience. Use plain language. Separate verified facts from assumptions. Cite official government sources with URLs and access dates. If you cannot verify a claim, label it unverified rather than guessing."

## Prompt 2 — Verify helpline and reporting steps
"Review the following draft reporting instructions for India. Check every helpline, URL, sequence and deadline against current official Government of India sources. Return a table with: claim, official source, exact supporting text or summary, verification date, status (verified / uncertain / incorrect), and correction. Do not invent a deadline. Draft: [paste non-sensitive draft]."

## Prompt 3 — Legal provision audit
"Explain only the following selected provisions of the Information Technology Act, 2000 using the latest official India Code text: [sections]. For each, give the section title, plain-language summary, official source URL and verification date. Do not give legal advice, infer that a provision applies to a specific person, or invent penalties. Flag amendments and uncertainty."

## Prompt 4 — Create a five-situation checklist
"Create a defensive, victim-centred checklist for [fictional incident type] in India. Include immediate safety, account protection, evidence preservation and official reporting. Include only verified government reporting channels. Do not promise money recovery. Do not ask for passwords, OTPs, PINs, CVVs or full identity numbers. Avoid attack instructions."

## Prompt 5 — Hallucination and safety audit
"Audit this AI-generated cybercrime guide. Identify wrong/outdated helplines, invented legal sections, unsupported deadlines, inaccurate portal steps, false certainty, victim-blaming, unsafe evidence handling and privacy risks. For each issue, quote the claim briefly, explain the risk and suggest a source-backed correction. If a claim cannot be verified, mark it uncertain."

## Prompt 6 — Awareness article
"Draft a plain-language cybercrime awareness article for Indian college students. Cover common cybercrime types, immediate actions, official reporting, evidence preservation, selected legal awareness and safe digital habits. Avoid sensationalism, unverified statistics and legal conclusions. Include citations for factual claims."

## Prompt 7 — Social media awareness
"Create five concise cyber-safety posts for students in India. Use accurate, non-alarmist language. Include official reporting channels only when verified. Do not use real brands, victim details, suspicious links or invented statistics."

## Prompt 8 — Quiz generation
"Generate ten multiple-choice questions from this verified cybercrime awareness guide. Each question must have one unambiguous correct answer and a short explanation. Do not add facts that are not in the source text. Provide an answer key and cite the relevant source or guide section."

## Prompt 9 — Compare AI tools
"Compare your answer with the same question answered by other AI tools. Score factual accuracy, completeness, source quality, currency, safety and clarity from 1–5. Identify every unsupported legal or procedural claim. Do not award points for confident wording without evidence."

## Prompt 10 — Responsible AI review
"Review this project for privacy, fairness, accessibility, victim sensitivity, legal-risk and cybersecurity misuse. Identify what data should never be entered, what decisions require a human and what limitations must be shown to users."
