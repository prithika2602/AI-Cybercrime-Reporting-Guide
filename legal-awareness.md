# Basic Cyber-Law Awareness (India)

This section is an introductory study aid. It does not decide whether an offence occurred or which law applies to a particular person. Police, prosecutors and courts assess the facts and applicable law.

## Selected Information Technology Act, 2000 provisions

- **Section 66 — Computer-related offences:** Addresses specified acts under Section 43 when done dishonestly or fraudulently.
- **Section 66C — Identity theft:** Covers fraudulent or dishonest use of another person's electronic signature, password or other unique identification feature.
- **Section 66D — Cheating by personation using a computer resource:** Addresses cheating by pretending to be another person through a communication device or computer resource.
- **Section 66E — Violation of privacy:** Addresses specified capture, publication or transmission of images of a person's private area without consent in circumstances set out in the provision.
- **Section 67 — Obscene material in electronic form:** Addresses publishing or transmitting obscene material in electronic form.
- **Section 67A — Sexually explicit material in electronic form:** Addresses publishing or transmitting material containing sexually explicit acts in electronic form.
- **Section 67B — Material depicting children in sexually explicit acts:** Addresses specified electronic material involving children. Do not save, copy, download or forward such material; report it through official channels.

## Important interpretation cautions

1. A single incident may involve more than one law, or a different law entirely.
2. The section names above are simplified descriptions, not complete statutory text.
3. Definitions, exceptions, amendments, penalties and procedural requirements must be checked in the latest official legislation.
4. Do not rely on AI to invent a section number, penalty, limitation period or legal conclusion.
5. Obtain advice from a qualified legal professional for a real case.
