# Six-Slide Presentation — Cybercrime Reporting Guide

## Slide 1 — Title and objective
**AI Cybercrime Reporting and Cyber Law Awareness Guide**
- Objective: help people identify cybercrime, preserve evidence and reach official reporting channels.
- Audience: students and general public in India.
- Scope: education and awareness; not a complaint-submission service.

## Slide 2 — Common cybercrime situations
- Online financial fraud / UPI fraud
- Account hacking
- Identity theft and impersonation
- Online harassment and threats
- Non-consensual sharing of private content

## Slide 3 — First response
- Stop further loss; call 1930 immediately for financial cyber fraud.
- Contact the bank/payment provider through official channels.
- Secure compromised accounts and enable MFA.
- Preserve original evidence and note a timeline.

## Slide 4 — Official reporting and legal awareness
- National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal: https://cybercrime.gov.in/
- Follow current portal instructions and keep acknowledgement details.
- Selected IT Act provisions: 66, 66C, 66D, 66E, 67, 67A and 67B.
- Legal summaries are not legal advice; verify current official legislation.

## Slide 5 — AI workflow and responsible use
- Research with multiple AI tools.
- Draft awareness material and incident checklists.
- Verify every helpline, legal claim and reporting instruction against official sources.
- Record hallucinations, outdated information and unsafe advice.
- Never upload real victim data or secrets to AI tools.

## Slide 6 — Demonstration and conclusion
- Select an incident in the interactive checklist builder.
- Copy/download the action checklist.
- Complete the 10-question quiz.
- Show source register, prompt library and verification log.
- Key message: **Pause. Verify. Preserve. Report.**
